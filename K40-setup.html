K40 wiring and GRBL-LPC setup

K40 CO₂ laser cutter · MKS SBASE v1.3 with LPC1769 · GRBL-LPC · LightBurn

As-wired connections

SignalConnection and function
Firing gateP2.5 drives the BED low-side MOSFET. BED negative connects to the laser PSU’s active-low L input. P2.5 high turns Q4 on and pulls L low. BED positive is on the board supply rail and must stay disconnected from L.
BED indicator branchR30 and D14 were both removed, opening the series path from VIN0 to Q4’s drain. The BED indicator is disabled.
Power commandJ8 pin 3 (P1.23) feeds the level shifter’s low-voltage input. Its high-voltage output goes to PSU IN, formerly connected to the wiper of the removed 5 kΩ potentiometer.
Level shifter supplyPSU 5V supplies the shifter’s high-voltage side. The low-voltage side can be supplied from either of two sources:
  • J8 pin 6 (P4.28), the currently wired connection. The firmware drives P4.28 high as a GPIO output at boot (LEVEL_SHIFTER_SUPPLY_P4_28). A GPIO can source about 4 mA at near 3.3 V, which suits BSS138-type modules (about 0.33 mA per channel through the 10 kΩ pull-ups) and TXS/TXB-type shifters. The supply is only present while this firmware is running. It is off during power-up, reset and the bootloader, and with stock firmware.
  • J2 pin 2, the board’s regulated 3.3 V rail. This is always on and independent of firmware, and is the preferred source for heavier loads. Confirm connector orientation before moving the wire.
Earlier firmware left P4.28 as an input, so the shifter was fed only through the weak internal pull-up (about 50 µA). That can sag when PWM is low and leave PSU IN partly high.
Signal groundJ8 pin 1 (DGND), shifter ground and PSU signal ground are common.
InterlocksThe lid and water-flow switches are hardwired to the PSU’s WP protection circuit.

Local GRBL-LPC customisations

The checkout is based on cprezzi/grbl-LPC commit 72d029e and reports 1.1f.20171008. The local firmware artifact was verified to use P1.23 for PWM and P2.5 for the firing gate. The exact image currently flashed to the controller has yet to be identified.

  • The build selects CPU_MAP_MKS_SBASE, DEFAULTS_K40 and SPINDLE_PWM_PIN_1_23. P1.23 is the primary output for PWM1 channel 4. Its secondary output is disabled, leaving P2.3 available for A-axis step. Build configuration · Pin map
  • USE_SPINDLE_DIR_AS_ENABLE_PIN uses the remapped direction pin P2.5 as the active-high firing gate. With SPINDLE_ENABLE_OFF_WITH_ZERO_SPEED disabled, the gate can remain asserted at S0 and during G0 while spindle mode is active. M5 clears the gate through the spindle-stop path. Spindle control
  • The latest source removes P1.23 from the MKS control-input mask and cycle-start decoder when it is selected for PWM. Serial cycle start (~) remains available. This change passed an ARM build. Controller flashing and hardware verification of this change are pending. Control pin map · Control input decoder
  • An M0 pause or a GRBL Safety Door hold releases the firing gate. On resume, the split-output firmware now waits until the stepper loads the first segment above the configured PWM off value before reasserting P2.5. The gate stays released through the pause and any initial travel or zero-power segments. Ordinary S0 handling after the gate is restored follows the existing spindle-enable configuration. Hold and resume · Spindle control · Segment execution
  • Local K40 defaults include 20 kHz PWM, 54% maximum duty and 320 × 230 mm nominal XY travel. All 46 compiled DEFAULTS_K40 values match K40_config.nc, including $3=1, $20=1, $23=3, $25=3000, $34=0, $35=11 and $36=54. These defaults matter because every SD-card flash resets the settings to them (see Flashing). Settings stored on the controller persist across resets, power cycles and crashes. The 157.5 steps/mm XY value is calibrated, not the nominal 160. Compiled defaults
  • The MKS board uses a dedicated hardware reset input. P1.0 is excluded from the GPIO control mask, and P1.22 can still report its active-low state when it is the only configured control pin. P1.22 is the Safety Door input (ENABLE_SAFETY_DOOR_INPUT_PIN) and is left unconnected, because the lid and water interlocks go to the PSU. Its internal pull-up is now set explicitly in PINMODE3, so it reads as a closed door rather than relying on the reset default. If P1.22 is pulled low, Grbl reports the door open and blocks resume. Control pin map · Control input decoder
  • LEVEL_SHIFTER_SUPPLY_P4_28 configures P4.28 as a GPIO output driven high in system_init(), to power the level shifter’s low-voltage side from J8 pin 6. Comment it out if the shifter is moved to the J2 3.3 V rail. Build configuration · System init
  • PWM settings writes reject nonfinite values, frequencies that would give an invalid timer period, and duty cycles outside 0–100%. When the PWM is set up, the firmware also caps the minimum duty at the maximum and the off value at the minimum, so a mis-ordered $34/$35/$36 cannot invert the power curve. Stored values are not changed. Settings handler · Spindle control

RC filter on PSU IN (fitted 18 November 2026)

This smooths the 20 kHz PWM into DC, as the original potentiometer supplied. Time constant 1 ms; ripple under 1%. Switch the K40 off at the mains before wiring.

Parts as built: a 2.2 kΩ series resistor and a 0.47 µF film capacitor (non-polarised), giving τ ≈ 1 ms. 1 kΩ with 1 µF (105) is equivalent. Keep the series resistor at 1 kΩ or more, because the capacitor discharges back through the shifter FET into P1.23. With 2.2 kΩ the pin current stays about 3.8 mA, within its 4 mA rating. Substitutes: 2.2 µF with 1 kΩ, or ten 0.1 µF (104) in parallel with 1 kΩ. Electrolytics need + to IN.

  1. Move the 2.2 kΩ pull-up from the PSU terminals to the level shifter, between HV (third pin on the top row) and HV1. It must be before the 1 kΩ, or "off" cannot reach 0 V.
  2. Take the HV1 wire out of PSU IN. Join it to one leg of the 2.2 kΩ series resistor and put the other leg into IN.
  3. Fit the 0.47 µF across the PSU IN and GND terminals.

Flashing the firmware

The MKS SBASE uses an SD-card bootloader. Flash build/firmware.bin. Do not flash build/grbl.hex: it overwrites the bootloader, which then needs a hardware programmer to restore.

Before flashing

  1. Disable the laser at the PSU (laser switch or test/enable switch). New builds can change gate and power-command behaviour at boot, so nothing should be able to fire until it is checked.
  2. Send $$ in the LightBurn console and save the output. K40_config.nc is the reference copy.

Flashing

  1. Power the board down and remove the microSD card. GRBL-LPC does not expose the card over USB, so use a card reader.
  2. Back up any existing FIRMWARE.CUR from the card. It is the image currently on the board and the way back if needed.
  3. Copy build/firmware.bin to the card root, named exactly firmware.bin. The card must be FAT32.
  4. Eject the card cleanly, refit it and power up. The LEDs flash for a few seconds while the bootloader writes the image.
  5. Remove the card again and check that firmware.bin has been renamed to FIRMWARE.CUR. If firmware.bin is still there, the flash did not happen. The usual causes are a wrong file name or a non-FAT32 card.

After flashing, with the laser still disabled

  1. Connect in LightBurn and confirm the start-up line shows Grbl 1.1f.
  2. Send $$ and compare with K40_config.nc. Flashing always resets settings to the compiled defaults. Settings are stored in flash sector 15 (0xF000), and firmware.bin fills that gap with zeros, so the bootloader overwrites them. A build from this repository has defaults matching K40_config.nc, so $$ should match without changes. If any value differs, send those lines from K40_config.nc through the console. Coordinate offsets (G54 to G59, G28/G30) and startup lines ($N) are also cleared. Re-enter them if you use them.
  3. Measure J8 pin 6 to DGND. It should read about 3.3 V from the P4.28 supply.
  4. Jog the axes to confirm motion and motor current are normal.

With the laser enabled, at low power

  1. Zero-power test: run M3 S0 with a G1 move and a G0 move. The mA meter should stay at zero.
  2. Supply under load: fire at a low S value and confirm J8 pin 6 stays close to 3.3 V.
  3. M0 test: run a short job containing M0. The laser should stay off through the pause and come back only on the next powered cut, never during a travel move.

Rollback: copy the saved FIRMWARE.CUR back to the card as firmware.bin and power-cycle.

Operating notes

  • The setup runs and the signal levels and PWM were checked in January 2026. Intermittent re-ignition after M0 prompted the resume fix. The rebuilt firmware image includes it. The fix still needs validation on the machine.
  • After flashing the P4.28 build, measure J8 pin 6 to DGND while firing at low power. It should hold close to 3.3 V.
  • For an affected cut, check that the saved G-code restores a positive S on the cutting move. If it does, comparing M4 with Constant Power (M3) on that layer can help determine whether speed-scaled power during acceleration contributes to the missed start. G-code parser · Motion and PWM updates
  • Tube current measured with laser_pulse_test.sh on 18 November 2026 (stationary pulse, analogue meter): S1000 at $36=68 gave 15–16 mA, and the firing threshold at 17.75% gave about 4.25 mA. That is roughly 0.22 mA per % duty, so $36=68 sat at the typical K40 continuous rating of about 15 mA. Those figures were taken with the weak level-shifter drive. After the 2.2 kΩ fix, $36=54 gives 15 mA. See the unstable-output note. $36 is the only current ceiling, because the PSU has no independent limit. The exact PSU and level-shifter models, and their behaviour across reset and power loss, remain unverified.
  • Unstable output (open issue, 18 November 2026). At the same S value, the tube flips between a noisy, weak discharge and a quiet, strong one. Above about 45%, a loud screech came with the sharpest lines and some quiet lines were wide. This happens at both 5 kHz and 20 kHz PWM. Mirrors, cooling and the visible state of the tube were checked and look fine. Measurements at S600 (47.6% duty) with laser_pulse_test.sh:
    • The firing gate switches cleanly: 14 mV idle, 4.28 V during the pulse.
    • PSU 5V holds 4.99 V during firing. DGND and PSU signal ground are bonded with low resistance.
    • Level-shifter output to PSU IN: 2.28 V with IN disconnected, 2.10 V connected with the laser disabled, and 1.84 V connected with the laser enabled. The shifter cannot hold the command against the PSU input, which looks like about 115 kΩ disarmed and about 40 kΩ armed (estimated from the module’s 10 kΩ pull-up). The module is the common 4-channel bidirectional MOSFET shifter, with SOT-23 FETs marked J1 (BSS138) and 10 kΩ pull-ups on both sides. The FET drives HV low strongly, but the high level comes only through the 10 kΩ pull-up. So the command depends on the PSU's own input state.
    Interim fix: solder 2.2 kΩ between the module’s HV pin (third pin on the top row, wired to PSU 5V) and whichever HV1–HV4 pin is wired to PSU IN. Its partner LV1–LV4 pin is the one wired to J8 pin 3. This sits in parallel with the on-board 10 kΩ. Fitting it across the PSU 5V and IN terminals is equivalent while there is no filter. If the RC filter is added, the pull-up must sit on the module side of the 1 kΩ series resistor. Otherwise the 2.2 kΩ/1 kΩ divider holds IN at about 1.6 V when the output should be low, and the tube can fire at "off". This gives about 1.8 kΩ of high-side drive. Do not go below 2.2 kΩ, because that pull-up current returns through the FET into P1.23, which is rated for about 4 mA. Interim fix fitted 18 November 2026, with the 2.2 kΩ across the PSU 5V and IN terminals. IN at S600 now reads 2.39 V disarmed and 2.32 V armed and firing (−3%, previously −12%). The command is about 26% stronger when firing, so $36 was lowered from 68 to 54, which gives 15 mA at S1000. The threshold dropped to 11.88%, so $35 went from 17 to 11, and lines above the threshold now look consistent. The flipping persists. In repeated card_test.sh runs at ENGRAVE_S=250 (21.75% duty, 3000 mm/min, 15 mm lines), lines often start weak and switch to strong partway through, after roughly 15–270 ms. This happens after an M0 resume and equally after a plain M5 + G4 P10 pause (TESTS=dwell), and also on the first line of a run. Travel after M0 never marked. So the M0 resume code is not the cause, and the controller signal is steady (gate clean, IN stiff). The remaining suspects are tube ageing (gas depletion) or the PSU failing to establish the discharge cleanly. Next steps: watch the mA needle during 3 s pulses (S=400 SECS=3 ./laser_pulse_test.sh) to see whether the current jumps when the output does, fit the RC filter to rule out PWM ripple, then a PSU or tube swap. Follow it with a 1 kΩ series resistor to IN and 1 µF from IN to PSU signal ground. Afterwards, re-check the IN voltage armed and disarmed, re-run pwm_min_test.sh and laser_pulse_test.sh, and re-tune $35 and $36. If the instability remains with a stiff, filtered command, suspect the PSU or the tube. Driving L with PWM while holding IN at a fixed current is the alternative.
